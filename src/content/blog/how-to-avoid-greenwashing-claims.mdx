**Adding checkable detail to the same green claim cut perceived greenwashing by about 40%.** In a 2026 study, 400 people rated four versions of one carbon label. The claim never changed. Only the detail behind it did. Ratings fell from 5.03 to 3.02 on the greenwashing scale.

Perceived greenwashing means how strongly readers believe a claim is misleading. The lesson is simple: specificity protects you, both with readers and with regulators.

---

## The Checklist

### Do

1. **State the number, the scope and the year.** "42% recycled polyester in the shell fabric, 2026 line," not "made with recycled materials."
2. **Say which part of the product the claim covers.** If the cap and label aren't recycled, say so.
3. **Call a future target a goal, and publish the steps.** Name what you'll do, by when, and how you'll report progress.
4. **Use third-party certifications, and name them.** "Fair Trade Certified" or "B Corp" can be checked. A logo you designed yourself can't.
5. **Keep a substantiation file for every claim.** If someone asks "how do you know that," you should be able to send the document the same day.

### Don't

6. **Don't use "sustainable," "eco" or "green" as the whole claim.** Use the fact underneath it instead.
7. **Don't say "recyclable" unless people can actually recycle it.** Chasing arrows on film that most facilities reject is the most common US case.
8. **Don't call a product carbon neutral because you bought offsets.** This is now banned for products sold to EU consumers.
9. **Don't let one green feature stand in for the whole business.** One recycled product line doesn't make a "sustainable company."
10. **Don't publish a claim no one in the company would sign.** If nobody can answer "how do we know," it isn't ready.

> **What this checklist is for:** reviewing environmental claims before they go on packaging, ads, product pages or sustainability pages.
> **Who it's for:** marketing, brand and sustainability leads, and founders who write their own copy.
> **Key sources:** a 2026 study in the *Journal of Sustainable Marketing* (n = 400), the [Greenwashing Enforcement Tracker](/blog/greenwashing-enforcement-tracker/) (2024 to 2026 cases), the FTC Green Guides (2012) and the EU EmpCo Directive (applies from September 27, 2026).

## The Case Behind Each Item

| # | Checklist item | The case that shows why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | State the number | H&M's hangtags claiming 55% and 56% recycled polyester were specific enough to be tested in court. That cut both ways: the claims could be checked, so the suit over them was allowed to proceed. |
| 2 | Name the scope | Coca-Cola had to state that its "100% recycled plastic bottle" excluded the cap and label (EU consumer authorities, May 2025). |
| 3 | Goal plus steps | JBS paid $1.1 million to New York. It must now call its 2040 target a "goal" and describe the specific steps it's taking (November 2025). |
| 3 | Goal plus steps | Tyson settled over "net zero by 2050" and "climate-smart beef." It can't make those claims for five years without expert verification (November 2025). |
| 6 | No standalone adjectives | The UK ASA banned Nike, Lacoste and Superdry ads that used "sustainable" on its own (December 2025). |
| 7 | Recyclable means recyclable | California's attorney general has collected $5.1 million from seven plastic bag makers over "recyclable" claims (2025 to 2026). |
| 8 | No offset-based neutrality | A Frankfurt court banned Apple's "CO2-neutral" watch claim. The offset project's land leases weren't secured past 2029 (August 2025). |
| 9 | No whole-business halo | The EU's EmpCo Directive bans claims about an entire product or business that are true for only one part (from September 2026). |

## Why Specificity Works

The 2026 study tested an oat-milk carbon label four ways. The vaguest said only "Climate-smart Product." The most detailed added a QR code and a plain description of the farming practices behind the number.

Perceived greenwashing dropped about 40%. Brand evaluation rose from 4.22 to 4.93. The effect held even after controlling for how much people knew about carbon. The detail itself did the work, not the education.

Regulators reward the same thing. A specific claim can be checked. A vague one invites the question "compared to what?" For how to phrase specific claims without sounding preachy, see [The Sustainable Brand Voice](/blog/the-sustainable-brand-voice/). ## The Rules Behind the Checklist

- **United States:** the FTC Green Guides (last updated 2012) remain the reference for deceptive environmental claims. State attorneys general and private lawsuits do most of the enforcement.
- **European Union:** the EmpCo Directive has applied since September 27, 2026. It bans generic environmental claims without recognized excellent performance, uncertified sustainability labels and offset-based "climate neutral" product claims. Future performance claims need a detailed plan verified by an independent expert. [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/fr/insights/publications/2026/10/the-eu-directive-on-empowering-consumers-for-the-green-transition-two-years-on-what-it-requires-and-where-the-member-states-stand)
- **United Kingdom:** the ASA enforces the CAP Code, as the Nike, Lacoste and Superdry rulings show.

For where every rule stands right now, see the [ESG Regulatory Status Tracker](/blog/esg-regulatory-status-tracker/).
