<p>How to pay a grant writer? When it comes to paying for grant writing services, there are a few different options available. One option is to use grant funds. While this may seem an ideal method, it's not an ethical option. <a href="https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/grant-writing-fees"><strong>The American Grant Writers' Association</strong></a> has guidelines that state grant writers should not accept compensation based on a percentage of the amount of the awarded grant funds (American Grant Writers' Association 2013). Grant writing for nonprofits can and is very rewarding. It can be done both part time and full time. You can work in a small town, on island, or in a big city. It is very flexible for remote work.</p><p>Also, check out <a href="__GHOST_URL__/blog/grant-writing-vs-proposal-writing/" rel="noreferrer">Grant Proposal Writing</a>.</p><figure class="kg-card kg-image-card"><img src="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/2025/08/how-to-pay-a-grant-writer-format-original.jpg" class="kg-image" alt="" loading="lazy" width="2000" height="1273" srcset="__GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w600/2025/08/how-to-pay-a-grant-writer-format-original.jpg 600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1000/2025/08/how-to-pay-a-grant-writer-format-original.jpg 1000w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w1600/2025/08/how-to-pay-a-grant-writer-format-original.jpg 1600w, __GHOST_URL__/content/images/size/w2400/2025/08/how-to-pay-a-grant-writer-format-original.jpg 2400w" sizes="(min-width: 720px) 720px"></figure><p>Paying a grant writer using grant funding is essentially the same thing as paying a grant writer with a percentage of the awarded grant funds, which makes it unethical. <a href="https://www.instrumentl.com/blog/grant-writing-fees"><strong>Another option is</strong></a><strong>, a better option, is </strong>to pay a flat fee, which varies based on the grant writer's experience, expertise, and service level.</p><p>The third option is to pay by the hour or by the page. While this option is the most common and affordable way to hire a grant writer, it can be dangerous because it encourages the writer to rush through the grant application in order to obtain higher payment. You may not get as good a service as you hope to get.</p><p>Grant writers often are <a href="https://www.justwritegrants.com/post/percentagepay"><strong>paid a flat fee</strong>,</a> which is generally around $25 to $75 per hour (Scotti and Saklofske 2017). This cost will vary depending on the writer's experience and the level of services you require, as well as the complexity and length of the project. It's important to work with an experienced <a href="__GHOST_URL__/blog/nonprofit-grant-writing/" rel="noreferrer">grant writer </a>who can successfully craft your application without having to rush through the writing process in order to meet their payment deadline.</p><p>Grant writers sometimes are <a href="https://work.chron.com/much-grant-writers-paid-per-grant-31075.html"><strong>paid a percentage of awarded grant funds,</strong></a> which could be considered unethical. According to the American Association of University Women, there are several reasons for this: For example, it could negatively impact the peer-review process by prompting reviewers to base their decisions on how much the grant writer will receive rather than the merits of the application (AAUW 2010).</p><p>Contingency <a href="https://www.nonprofitmarketingguide.com/avoiding-unethical-pay-structures-a-guide-for-grant-writers-nonprofit-professionals/"><strong>pay also</strong></a> is controversial because grant writers have no control over whether their organization receives a grant. The uncertainty involved can cause stress and financial hardship for both grant writers and their clients, so it's best to avoid contingency pay when possible. If you do choose to work with a grant writer who accepts contingency pay, make sure that you are comfortable with the terms of your agreement and that the payment will not compromise the quality of the work. Grant writing is not an exact science.</p><p>The <a href="https://www.thenonprofittimes.com/npt_articles/paying-commission-grant-proposals-dont/"><strong>American Association of University Women (AAUW)</strong></a> discourages contingency pay arrangements.
