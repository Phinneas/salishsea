**Last updated: October 15, 2026. Next update: January 2027.**

**California has now collected $5.1 million from seven plastic bag makers over "recyclable" claims.** That's the largest running total on this page. Recycling claims are still where most US enforcement lands.

Net-zero targets are the fastest-growing new category. In late 2025, JBS paid $1.1 million and Tyson agreed to drop "net zero" and "climate-smart" claims unless an expert verifies them.

> **What this tracker logs:** government enforcement actions, court rulings and major settlements over environmental marketing claims.
> **Coverage:** US federal, state and city actions, plus EU, UK, French, German and Australian actions that set a precedent for brands selling there.
> **Method:** each entry is checked against a primary source (press release, court record or regulator ruling) or at least one law-firm analysis of it. Entries are dated by ruling, settlement or filing date.
> **Update cadence:** quarterly. New cases are added at the top of the log.

---

## What the 2025 to 2026 Cases Show

1. **Recycling claims draw the most US enforcement.** California, Arizona and Philadelphia all went after "recyclable" labels on plastic film and bags.
2. **Net-zero targets now need a plan behind them.** Regulators didn't argue the targets were lies. They argued the companies had no realistic path to them.
3. **Offset-based "carbon neutral" claims split by jurisdiction.** The same Apple Watch claim lost in Germany and survived in a US federal court.
4. **Courts reward specific, testable claims and punish vague ones.** A hangtag with a percentage on it could be tested and challenged. Vague website copy got dismissed.
5. **The EU just raised the bar.** Since September 27, 2026, offset-based neutrality claims on products are banned for EU consumers.

## The Patterns, Case by Case

### Recycling claims: most of the US volume

The bag and film cases all follow one logic. The material might be recyclable somewhere. It isn't accepted by the facilities where the product is sold. California's SB 270 cases rest on that gap, and so do Arizona's Hefty settlement and Philadelphia's suit.

One caveat for 2026. A federal judge blocked California's newer SB 343 law in July, on vagueness and First Amendment grounds. That ruling is preliminary. It doesn't touch the SB 270 bag settlements, and it doesn't stop private lawsuits under other laws. Don't read it as an all-clear on recyclability claims.

### Net-zero targets: a goal needs a path

JBS and Tyson weren't punished for missing a target. They were challenged for announcing targets with no realistic plan, while production kept growing. The fixes were the same in both cases. Call it a goal. Say what you're actually doing. Get the claim verified.

### Carbon neutral: same claim, opposite outcomes

Apple's Apple Watch claim is the clearest split on this page. A German court banned it in August 2025 because the forestry offsets behind it weren't secured long term. A US federal court dismissed a challenge to the same claim in February 2026 because the plaintiffs couldn't show the offsets were miscounted.

Since September 27, 2026, the question is moot for EU consumers. The Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive bans product claims of neutral, reduced or positive climate impact that rely on offsets.

### Specific claims are easier to test, in both directions

H&M's hangtags named a percentage, so a shopper could test them. That's why those claims survived dismissal. The UK's Nike, Lacoste and Superdry rulings show the reverse. "Sustainable" with no qualifier promised more than the real data behind it.

The lesson isn't to avoid specifics. It's to make sure every specific number holds up. For why regulators stopped asking about intent, see [what greenwashing actually is](/blog/what-greenwashing-actually-is/).
