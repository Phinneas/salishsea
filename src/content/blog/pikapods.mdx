<p>Setting up a Ghost blog hosted on Pikapods while using an AWS-registered domain involves several key steps. This guide walks you through the entire process, from domain configuration to testing your setup. By the end, you’ll see just how simple it is to set up your own Ghost blog and make it accessible through your custom domain, ready to share your content with the world.</p><h2 id="have-your-accounts-ready">Have Your Accounts Ready</h2><p>To get started, you'll need a few things: an AWS-registered domain, access to Route 53, a Pikapods account, and a basic understanding of DNS records like A, CNAME, and Alias.</p><h3 id="create-a-hosted-zone-in-aws-route-53">Create a Hosted Zone in AWS Route 53</h3><ol><li>Navigate to the <strong>Route 53</strong> service in your AWS Management Console. This step is essential because creating a hosted zone is the starting point for managing DNS records for your domain. Without it, you won’t be able to configure the necessary records to connect your domain to Pikapods.</li><li>Under <strong>Hosted Zones</strong>, click <strong>Create Hosted Zone</strong>.</li><li>Enter your domain name (e.g., <code>yourdomain.com</code>) and select <strong>Public Hosted Zone</strong>.</li><li>Note the <strong>Name Server (NS)</strong> records generated. These need to replace the name servers for your domain in AWS or your domain registrar.</li></ol><h3 id="update-domain-s-name-servers">Update Domain’s Name Servers</h3><ol><li>Go to the <strong>Route 53 Registered Domains</strong> section or your AWS or external domain registrar.</li><li>Update the name servers to the ones provided by the hosted zone you created. Copy the nameservers listed for the hosted zone over to your domain. They must match or this just won’t work.</li><li>Save the changes and allow time for DNS propagation (this can take a few minutes to several hours).</li></ol><h3 id="set-up-a-cloudfront-distribution">Set Up a CloudFront Distribution</h3><p>To route traffic for your root domain, create a CloudFront distribution:</p><!--kg-card-begin: html--><ol data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Open the <strong>CloudFront</strong> service in AWS.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Click <strong>Create Distribution</strong> and configure:</p><!--kg-card-begin: html--><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Origin Domain Name</strong>: The Pikapods domain (e.g., <code>your-unique-blog.pikapod.net</code>). Which was copied from PikaPods.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Default Cache Behavior</strong>:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Viewer Protocol Policy: Redirect HTTP to HTTPS.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Allowed HTTP Methods: GET, HEAD (or include others as needed).</p></li></ul></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Alternate Domain Names (CNAMEs)</strong>: Add your domain (e.g., <code>yourdomain.com</code> and <code>www.yourdomain.com</code>).</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>SSL/TLS Certificate</strong>: Use AWS Certificate Manager (ACM) to request a certificate for your domain.</p></li></ul><!--kg-card-end: html--></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Save and deploy the distribution. This may take several minutes as CloudFront processes the settings, provisions resources, and validates the SSL certificate (if configured). During this time, DNS propagation ensures that your domain points to the distribution.</p></li></ol><!--kg-card-end: html--><h3 id="configure-dns-records-for-pikapods">Configure DNS Records for Pikapods</h3><h4 id="for-www-yourdomain-com-">For <code>www.yourdomain.com</code>:</h4><!--kg-card-begin: html--><ol data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Go to your Route 53 Hosted Zone.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Create a <strong>CNAME Record</strong>:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Record Name</strong>: <code>www</code></p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Value</strong>: The domain provided by Pikapods (e.g., <code>your-unique-blog.pikapod.net</code>).</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>TTL</strong>: 300 seconds (default).</p></li></ul></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Save the record.</p></li></ol><!--kg-card-end: html--><h4 id="for-yourdomain-com-root-domain-this-is-the-a-record">For <code>yourdomain.com</code> (Root Domain): <strong>This is the A record</strong></h4><p>Root domains can't use a CNAME because they must resolve directly to an IP address, which CNAME records can't provide. An A record, or Alias record, works as a suitable workaround by allowing the root domain to behave like it has a CNAME while resolving to an AWS resource such as CloudFront. Instead, use an <strong>Alias Record</strong>:</p><!--kg-card-begin: html--><ol data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Create an <strong>A Record</strong>:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Record Name</strong>: Leave blank or <code>@</code> (indicates the root domain).</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Record Type</strong>: A – Routes traffic to an IPv4 address and some AWS resources.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Alias</strong>: Enable.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><strong>Route Traffic To</strong>: Select <strong>Alias to CloudFront distribution</strong>.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Choose your CloudFront distribution (see Previous Step).</p></li></ul></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Save the record.</p></li></ol><!--kg-card-end: html--><h3 id="verify-your-setup-in-pikapods">Verify Your Setup in Pikapods</h3><ol><li>In your Pikapods admin dashboard, navigate to the <strong>Domain</strong> section.</li><li>Enter your custom domain (e.g., <code>yourdomain.com</code>).</li><li>Pikapods will verify your DNS configuration. Ensure both the CNAME for <code>www</code> and the Alias for the root domain are working. If the verification fails, check whether your DNS records have fully propagated using tools like whatsmydns.net. You should also confirm that the CNAME and Alias records are correctly set up in Route 53.</li><li>Save your changes.</li></ol><h3 id="test-your-domain">Test Your Domain</h3><!--kg-card-begin: html--><ol data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Use tools like <a href="https://www.whatsmydns.net">whatsmydns.net</a> to check DNS propagation for:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">NS and SOA records (confirm Route 53 is authoritative).</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">CNAME records for <code>www.yourdomain.com</code>.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Alias/A records for <code>yourdomain.com</code>.</p></li></ul></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;">Open a browser and visit:</p><ul data-rte-list="default"><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><code>http://yourdomain.com</code> – It should redirect to <code>https://yourdomain.com</code>.</p></li><li><p class="" style="white-space:pre-wrap;"><code>http://www.yourdomain.com</code> – It should load directly over HTTPS.</p></li></ul></li></ol><!--kg-card-end: html--><h3 id="troubleshooting-tips">Troubleshooting Tips</h3><ul><li>If DNS changes are not working, double-check your Route 53 records and ensure the name servers match those in the domain registrar.</li><li>For SSL errors, ensure your CloudFront distribution includes a valid certificate for your domain.</li><li>If Pikapods cannot verify the root domain, confirm the Alias record points to the CloudFront distribution.</li></ul><p>With these steps, you’ve successfully connected your AWS-registered domain to a Ghost blog hosted on Pikapods. The setup ensures seamless redirection between <code>www</code> and the root domain, secure HTTPS access, and reliable DNS routing. Now you can start sharing your ideas with the world. Happy blogging!</p><p>Feel free to share this guide with others looking to set up their Ghost blogs with AWS and Pikapods!</p>
