**A full-time sustainability manager averages about $133,000 a year in US total pay.** For a $10M company, that's 1.3% of revenue before benefits, payroll taxes or software. Most companies this size need far less.

The number I use throughout this page is ESG cost as a share of revenue. It's the annual cost of an engagement model divided by $10M.

Four findings, strongest first:

1. **A customer request is the most common trigger.** CDP's supply chain members asked more than 45,000 suppliers to disclose environmental data in 2025.
2. **Project work answers most triggers.** One supplier data request typically costs $3,000 to $8,000, or 0.03% to 0.08% of revenue.
3. **Fractional help makes sense only with recurring work.** Retainers start around $3,000 to $8,000 a month.
4. **Enterprise carbon software is usually overkill.** Watershed's median contract is about $70,000 a year.

> **What this page answers:** whether outside ESG help is worth paying for below enterprise scale, and which model fits.
> **Who it's for:** founders, CFOs and operations leads at US companies with roughly $5M to $20M in revenue.
> **Key sources:** Glassdoor sustainability manager pay (US, 2026), Stealth Agents fractional CSO benchmarks (July 2026), Keslio published pricing (2026), Vendr Watershed deal data (2026), CDP supply chain program (2025 data), the EU Omnibus I Directive (in force March 18, 2026) and CARB's SB 253 regulation (October 2026).
> **Method:** annual cost for each model, divided by $10M revenue. Ranges are vendor-published or benchmark figures, not quotes. Your price will vary with scope.
> **Last checked:** November 2, 2026.

## The Short Answer

At $10M, buy ESG help narrowly and only when something triggers it. The usual triggers are a customer questionnaire, an RFP that scores sustainability, a certification you want or a product claim you need to defend.

Start with a one-time project. Move to a fractional lead only when the work repeats every year across several customers. A full-time hire rarely makes sense below $50M unless sustainability is the product.

## Five Ways to Buy ESG Help, Priced

| Model | Typical annual cost | Share of $10M revenue | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY with free tools | $0 plus staff time | Staff time only | No customer asking yet |
| One-time project | $3,000 to $8,000 per request | 0.03% to 0.08% | One questionnaire, one certification |
| Fractional retainer | $36,000 to $96,000 | 0.4% to 1.0% | Recurring reporting to several customers |
| Enterprise software + advisor | About $70,000 median, plus setup | 0.7% and up | Multi-entity companies with complex Scope 3 |
| Full-time manager | About $133,000 total pay, before benefits | 1.3% and up | Sustainability is core to the product or brand |

Sources: [Keslio pricing](https://www.keslio.com/pricing), [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-a-chief-sustainability-officer), [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/watershed), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/sustainability-manager-salary-SRCH_KO0,22.htm)

### DIY with free tools

This works when nobody is asking yet. You can build a rough footprint with free calculators and your utility and travel bills. The cost is staff time. The risk is that a rough number ends up in a customer response or a marketing claim it can't support.

### One-time project

This is the right default at $10M. Keslio prices supplier request support at $3,000 to $8,000. A single emissions number sits at the low end. A full CDP or EcoVadis submission sits at the high end. [Keslio](https://www.keslio.com/pricing)

A first greenhouse gas baseline for a small business has run roughly $4,000 to $10,000 in older benchmarks. [Work for Climate](https://www.workforclimate.org/actions/guide-carbon-measurement) Treat that as a floor. Messy data raises the price.

### Fractional retainer

Fractional sustainability leads work a set number of hours each month. Entry-level retainers run $4,000 to $8,000 a month for 6 to 10 hours a week. The mid tier, $8,000 to $14,000 a month, is aimed at $25M to $150M companies. [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-a-chief-sustainability-officer) Some firms start lower, around $3,000 a month. [Keslio](https://www.keslio.com/pricing)

At $10M, that's 0.4% to 1.0% of revenue. It only pays off if the same work comes back every year.

### Enterprise software plus an advisor

Watershed's median buyer pays about $70,031 a year, with a reported range of $20,619 to $158,138. Implementation can add $10,000 to $50,000 or more. [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/watershed) These platforms are built for multi-entity companies with complex supply chains. Most $10M companies won't use most of the features.

### Full-time manager

Glassdoor puts average US sustainability manager pay at $133,086, with a range of about $101,000 to $178,000. [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/sustainability-manager-salary-SRCH_KO0,22.htm) Add benefits and payroll taxes and you're well past 1.3% of revenue. That's a big bet for a company where sustainability is a support function.

## What Regulation Actually Requires at $10M

Very little, directly.

**California SB 253** requires Scope 1 and 2 reporting only from companies above $1 billion in revenue doing business in California. The first reports are due November 10, 2026. [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/insights/memo/2026/October/CARB-Initial-Regulation-Confirms-November-10-Reporting-Deadline), [Brightest](https://www.brightest.io/california-sb-253-sb-261-climate-corporate-disclosure-act)

**The EU's CSRD** now applies only to companies above 1,000 employees and €450 million in net turnover. The Omnibus I Directive took effect March 18, 2026. [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/EU-Sustainability-Omnibus-Published-in-the-Official-Journal)

The Omnibus also added a **value chain cap**. Companies with up to 1,000 employees can decline CSRD-driven requests that go beyond a voluntary standard based on EFRAG's VSME. Reporting companies must tell you which questions go beyond it. [Accountancy Europe](https://accountancyeurope.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/260129-Omnibus-explained-CSRD-factsheet.pdf)

So the pressure on a $10M company is indirect. It comes from:

- **Large customers** building their own inventories. CDP's 200+ supply chain members asked 45,000+ suppliers to disclose in 2025. [CDP](https://www.cdp.net/en/supply-chain)
- **Retail buyers and distributors** with supplier scorecards.
- **Public and institutional RFPs** that score sustainability.
- **Your own marketing claims.** Unsupported green claims are an enforcement risk at any size. See the [Greenwashing Enforcement Tracker](/blog/greenwashing-enforcement-tracker/).

For the full list of what applies to small brands, see [ESG reporting requirements for small sustainable brands](/blog/esg-reporting-requirements-for-small-sustainable-brands/).

## When Outside Help Pays for Itself

Run the math against the revenue at stake, not against your general sense of what ESG is worth.

1. **A customer data request tied to a contract.** If a $500,000 account sends a questionnaire, a $5,000 project is 1% of that account. That's usually an easy yes.
2. **An RFP that scores sustainability.** If sustainability is 10% of the score, a weak answer can cost you the bid.
3. **A certification you've decided to pursue.** B Corp and EcoVadis both reward organized evidence. See [B Corp Just Rewrote the Rulebook](/blog/b-corp-new-standards-2026/) for the 2026 standards.
4. **Product claims you make in public.** Paying to substantiate a claim costs less than defending a challenge to it.
5. **The same request from three or more customers a year.** At that point a fractional lead can cost less than repeated one-off projects.

## When It Doesn't

1. **No buyer is asking.** Track your own energy and travel data, and wait for a real trigger.
2. **You make no environmental claims.** There's nothing to substantiate yet.
3. **The only pressure is a general sense that you "should do ESG."** That's a reason to learn, not to sign a retainer.
4. **The consultant leads with software.** At $10M, you need answers before a platform.

## Self-Check: Which Model Fits?

Answer yes or no:

1. Has a customer, buyer or lender asked for emissions or ESG data in the last 12 months?
2. Is that request tied to revenue you'd lose without an answer?
3. Do you expect the same kind of request from three or more customers each year?
4. Do you make environmental claims on packaging, your website or in sales materials?
5. Is sustainability part of why customers buy from you?

**How to read it:**

- **No to all five:** DIY. Keep basic records.
- **Yes to 1 and 2 only:** one-time project.
- **Yes to 3, or to 4 and 5 together:** fractional help, or a project now with a plan to go fractional.
- **Yes to all five, with revenue growing past $25M:** price out a fractional lead against a full-time hire.
