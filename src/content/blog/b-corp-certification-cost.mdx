**B Corp certification costs $2,100 a year for a US brand under $5 million in revenue.** It takes about 12 months. And since 2026, you have to pass all seven of B Lab's Impact Topics, not just score well overall.

That makes it worth it for some small brands and a poor use of a year for others. The deciding factor usually isn't your values. It's your stage.

This guide covers the 2026 fees, what the new standards require, what the research says certification actually does for a small company, and how it compares with 1% for the Planet. It ends with a decision table you can use today.

---

## What B Corp Certification Costs in 2026

B Lab U.S. & Canada sets the annual certification fee by revenue. Here are the bands that apply to most small and mid-sized sustainable brands.

| Annual revenue | Annual certification fee (USD) |
|---|---|
| Under $5M | $2,100 |
| $5M to $7.5M | $3,150 |
| $7.5M to $10M | $3,938 |
| $10M to $15M | $6,300 |
| $15M to $20M | $8,925 |
| $20M to $30M | $12,600 |
| $30M to $50M | $16,800 |

A portion of that fee now covers an independent third-party audit. The audit is set by an accredited assurance provider based on your company's size and complexity. If your company needs extra verification work, because of a complex structure, a higher risk profile or problems the auditor finds, additional audit costs can apply.

The fee is the smallest part of the real cost. Three other costs matter more for a small team.

**Time.** Plan for at least 12 months from starting the assessment to certification. Someone on your team has to own that process the whole way through.

**Evidence.** The new standard needs documentation across all seven topics. A founder-run brand with no sustainability staff will feel this most.

**Ongoing checkpoints.** Certification now runs on a five-year cycle. More requirements switch on at Year 3 and again at Year 5. You're signing up for continued improvement, not a one-time badge.

## What the 2026 Standards Require

B Corp changed structurally in 2026. Much of the advice still online describes the old version.

Under the old B Impact Assessment, a company needed 80 out of 200 points. Strength in one area could cover weakness in another. Great packaging could offset weak labor practices.

The new standard removes that trade-off. Every company must meet minimum requirements in each of seven Impact Topics.

1. Purpose and Stakeholder Governance
2. Climate Action
3. Human Rights
4. Fair Work
5. Justice, Equity, Diversity and Inclusion
6. Environmental Stewardship and Circularity
7. Government Affairs and Collective Action

Climate Action is the one small brands most often underestimate. It requires a 1.5°C-aligned climate plan, and that applies at every company size. If you don't know your emissions baseline today, that's the first gap to close, whether or not you certify.

**A quick readiness check.** Before you start, ask whether you could show evidence today for each of these:

- A governance document that commits the company to stakeholders, not just shareholders
- A current emissions baseline and a reduction plan tied to 1.5°C
- Written labor and pay practices for your own staff
- Basic human rights due diligence on your key suppliers
- A materials, waste or circularity policy you actually follow

If most of those are blank, certification is a 12-month build, not a 12-month paperwork exercise. That isn't a reason to skip it. It's a reason to plan for it honestly.

## When B Corp Pays Off for a Small Brand

The strongest evidence on this comes from a 2025 *Journal of Business Ethics* study. Researchers compared 374 B Corp–certified sustainability ventures with 374 similar non-certified ones, using propensity score matching to isolate the effect of certification.

Certification measurably increased funding. But the effect was strongest for early-stage ventures and weakened as companies matured.

The reason is signaling. Certification works because it's costly, independently verified and easy for an investor to check. That matters most when a young company has little track record of its own. As a brand builds a history investors can look at directly, the badge does less of the work.

So the funding case for B Corp is front-loaded. If you're raising in the next year or two, the timing lines up. If you're profitable, not raising and selling direct to consumers, the funding benefit mostly doesn't apply to you.

The customer case has a catch too. A 2026 study in the *Journal of Sustainable Marketing* found that adding checkable detail to an identical sustainability label cut perceived greenwashing by about 40%. The same logic applies to a certification logo. A B Corp badge with no current number beside it reads like any other unverified claim. The badge plus your actual score, or your actual emissions figure, is what a skeptical buyer can't wave away. Our guide to [greenwashing examples](/blog/greenwashing-examples/) shows what happens when claims outrun the evidence behind them.

## The Lighter-Effort Option: 1% for the Planet

1% for the Planet asks for something narrower. Members give at least 1% of annual revenue to approved environmental nonprofits.

The rules are specific:

- **At least half must be cash.** Up to 50% can be non-monetary.
- **In-kind counts at set values.** Products at fair market value, services at your normal billable rate, volunteer time at $29.95 per hour per employee (or your average employee rate) and pre-approved ad spend that features 1% for the Planet.
- **Certification is annual.** Members certify within 120 days of fiscal year-end, with proof of revenue and proof of contributions.
- **Membership dues count toward the 1%.** Dues run from $500 a year under $500K revenue to $16,000 a year at $35 to 50 million. There's also a one-time activation fee of $100 to $1,000.

Here's the part most comparisons miss. **1% for the Planet takes far less effort than B Corp, but it usually costs more money.**

| Example brand | B Corp annual fee | 1% for the Planet commitment |
|---|---|---|
| $1M revenue | $2,100 | $10,000 (at least $5,000 cash) |
| $3M revenue | $2,100 | $30,000 (at least $15,000 cash) |
| $8M revenue | $3,938 | $80,000 (at least $40,000 cash) |

The B Corp fee doesn't include the staff time and audit work described above. The 1% figure includes dues. Still, the gap is real. B Corp costs you a year of effort. 1% for the Planet costs you a fixed share of revenue every year.

The signal is narrower too. 1% for the Planet tells a customer you give on schedule. It doesn't say anything about your supply chain, labor practices or governance.

| | B Corp (2026 standards) | 1% for the Planet |
|---|---|---|
| **What's assessed** | Governance, labor, human rights, climate plan, supply chain | Annual giving only |
| **Verification** | Independent third-party audit, five-year cycle | Annual review of revenue and donation proof |
| **Annual cost, small brand** | $2,100 to $3,938 under $10M revenue, plus staff time | 1% of revenue, at least half in cash |
| **Time to first certification** | About 12 months | One certification cycle |
| **Best fit** | Brands ready to build processes around a standard | Brands that want a simple, verifiable commitment now |

## Before You Certify: What's Actually Required

Neither certification is legally required. It's worth being clear about that, because a lot of small brands spend energy on the wrong risk.

The SEC's climate disclosure rule never applied to smaller reporting companies, and the SEC formally proposed rescinding it in May 2026. California's SB 253 and SB 261 apply to companies with $1 billion and $500 million or more in revenue.

The rule that does apply at every size is the one on marketing claims. Section 5 of the FTC Act, the FTC's Green Guides and state consumer-protection laws all prohibit environmental claims that mislead a reasonable consumer. There's no revenue floor. That's where small brands actually get caught, and it's why the claims on your label deserve attention before any certification does.

## Should You Get B Corp Certified? A Decision Table

| Your situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Raising capital in the next 1 to 2 years, and someone can own a 12-month process | Start B Corp now. The funding signal is strongest at your stage. |
| Want B Corp, but the readiness check came back mostly blank | Prep first. Build your emissions baseline and governance docs this year. Certify next year. |
| Not raising, small team, want a verifiable commitment this year | Join 1% for the Planet. Budget for the full 1%, with at least half in cash. |
| Not raising, and margins can't absorb 1% of revenue | Neither for now. Audit your marketing claims and publish specific, checkable numbers instead. |

Whatever you choose, put a specific number next to the badge. This year's score, this year's donation total or this year's emissions figure. That's what turns a logo into evidence.
