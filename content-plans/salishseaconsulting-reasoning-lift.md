# Content Plan: Reasoning Lift Applied to Salish Sea Consulting
**Source research:** Kevin Indig, Growth Memo — *Reasoning Lift* (May 18, 2026)
**Site:** salishseaconsulting.com — conscious marketing, conversion copywriting, grant writing for sustainable brands
**Audience:** Sustainable brands, nonprofits, and mission-driven organizations needing marketing and funding support

---

## Core Thesis

> Salish Sea Consulting serves two buyer journeys — grant writing and sustainable brand marketing — neither of which has Problem-stage content, which is the exact gap high-reasoning AI exploits to persist citations all the way to a hiring decision.

---

## Vertical Reality Check

| Service line | Closest vertical | Delta |
|---|---|---|
| Grant writing | Finance (+28pp) | Regulatory docs, compliance, foundation guidelines |
| Sustainable brand marketing | Health/Lifestyle (+24pp) | Values-driven decisions, authoritative research |
| Web/copywriting tools | B2B SaaS (+16pp) | Workflow, integration, documentation |

Grant writing is the highest-leverage vertical. Grant applications are regulatory documents with compliance requirements, funder-specific guidelines, and IRS reporting obligations — exactly the complexity that causes high reasoning to seek authoritative primary sources.

## Pillar 1 — Two Buyer Journeys, Both Missing Stage 1

**Grant writing buyer journey:**
1. **Problem:** *"Our grant applications keep getting rejected"*
2. **Exploration:** *"What makes a successful grant application?"*
3. **Comparison:** *"Hire a grant writer vs. do it ourselves"*
4. **Validation:** *"Is this consultant right for our type of organization?"*
5. **Selection:** *"How does working with Salish Sea Consulting actually work?"*

**Sustainable brand marketing buyer journey:**
1. **Problem:** *"Our sustainable brand isn't converting — worse brands with better marketing are winning"*
2. **Exploration:** *"Why doesn't conventional marketing work for mission-driven brands?"*
3. **Comparison:** *"Specialist vs. generalist copywriter for a sustainable brand"*
4. **Validation:** *"Does this consultant actually understand our mission?"*
5. **Selection:** *"What does the engagement look like and what does it cost?"*

| Format | Title | Core angle |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Blog | "Why Grant Applications Get Rejected (It's Usually Not What You Think)" | Stage 1 (Problem) for the grant writing journey. Most rejections come from narrative failures, not eligibility gaps. Frames Chester as the expert before the reader knows they need a grant writer. |
| Blog | "Why Sustainable Brand Marketing Underperforms — and Why It's Not Your Fault" | Stage 1 (Problem) for the marketing journey. Conventional conversion tactics feel misaligned for mission-driven brands because they are — conscious consumers respond to different signals. |
| Blog | "What Does a Foundation Actually Look for in a Grant Application?" | Stage 2 (Exploration). Sources foundation program officer research and IRS compliance requirements. Gov/academic sources quadruple under high reasoning — structure this like a reference document. |

## Pillar 2 — Grant Writing as Regulatory Content (The Finance Angle)

Grant writing gets +28pp lift under high reasoning because foundations publish specific guidelines, the IRS governs 501(c)(3) compliance, and federal grants (NSF SBIR, EPA, USDA) have formal requirements.

**What sub-queries fire on "should I hire a grant writer":**
- Grant writing success rate with vs. without professional help
- Cost of grant writer vs. value of grant won
- Funder-specific requirements (federal vs. foundation vs. corporate)
- IRS compliance in grant narratives
- Logic model requirements
- Funder interview process
- 12-18 month grant calendar structure

| Format | Title | Core angle |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Evergreen guide | "Federal Grant Application Requirements: What NSF, EPA, and USDA Actually Want" | Structures federal grant requirements as a reference document. Gov sources quadruple under high reasoning — puts Salish Sea in the same citation tier as agency sites. |
| Evergreen guide | "The Real Cost of Grant Writing: DIY vs. Professional vs. What You Actually Win" | Data-led analysis of ROI on professional grant writing. Specific numbers, not platitudes. Makes the build-vs-buy case with explicit cost ranges. |
| Structured reference | "Foundation Grant Requirements by Funder Type: A Field Guide" | Community foundations vs. corporate foundations vs. family foundations — what each expects, what each rejects. Citable as a primary reference. |
| Case study | "How a [Sustainable Brand / Environmental Nonprofit] Won a [$X] Grant" | Validation-stage content. Narrative case study with specific numbers, timeline, and narrative strategy. |
| FAQ | "Can Grant Funds Pay for Grant Writing?" | High-volume query. Restructure as a direct FAQ with specific IRS guidance and funder policy citations. |

## Pillar 3 — The Integration Positioning (The Differentiation Play)

The core differentiator — one person handles both copy and technology — is stated on the homepage but not supported by any content. High reasoning fires 24 sub-queries at Comparison stage; Salish Sea has no content structured to win them.

**What sub-queries fire on "sustainable brand marketing consultant":**
- What does a conversion copywriter do vs. a web developer
- Cost of separate copywriter + developer vs. integrated
- How marketing automation connects to copy
- SEO copywriting for sustainable brands specifically
- What "conscious consumer" marketing looks like vs. conventional

| Format | Title | Core angle |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Blog | "The Hidden Cost of Misaligned Copywriters and Developers (For Sustainable Brands)" | Stage 1-2. Names the exact problem the homepage positions against. Specific: what goes wrong, what it costs, what the fix looks like. |
| Blog | "Conscious Consumer vs. Conventional Buyer: Why Your Marketing Tactics Feel Wrong" | Stage 2. Research-backed explanation of how conscious consumers make decisions differently. References behavioral economics and sustainable consumption research. |
| Comparison guide | "Generalist Copywriter vs. Sustainable Brand Specialist: What's Actually Different" | Stage 3. Structured comparison covering values-driven purchase decisions, sustainable certifications, grant narrative overlap, Pacific Northwest market knowledge. |
| Long-form | "How Conversion Copywriting Works for Mission-Driven Organizations" | Stage 2 (Exploration). Explains the integrated copy + tech + automation system. Biggest gap — no content explains the "custom marketing tools" piece at all. |

## Pillar 4 — Regional & Sector Specificity (Pacific Northwest, Sustainable Brands)

The site promises Pacific Northwest expertise but has no content that delivers on it specifically. High reasoning fires sub-queries about regional context, sector-specific regulations, and local funding landscapes.

| Format | Title | Core angle |
|--------|-------|------------|
| Regional guide | "Grant Funding for Pacific Northwest Environmental Nonprofits: A Funder Directory" | Local foundations (Russell Family, Bullitt, Meyer Memorial Trust), state programs, and federal opportunities specific to WA/OR. Makes Salish Sea the regional authority. |
| Blog | "Sustainable Brand Marketing in the Pacific Northwest: What Works Here That Doesn't Work Elsewhere" | Regional specificity is a sub-query magnet. The Pacific Northwest conscious consumer is a specific archetype. |
| Blog | "Oregon and Washington Nonprofit Registration: What the Process Actually Looks Like" | PNW-specific version of the existing generic post. Targets the exact regional audience and maps to state-level regulatory sources. |
